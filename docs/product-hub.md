# Clinic Growth System — Product & Research Hub

## Purpose

This is the main working document for the project. It links the original business plan, external validation, our interpretation, and our development decisions.

## 1. Source material

### Original PDF

The original document is an internal operating plan and founder/intern execution manual. Its core thesis is to start as a specialized, productized clinic-growth service, prove that private clinics will pay for measurable improvements, and **not build software first**. The initial wedge is dermatology/aesthetic dermatology in Mumbai, targeting owner-led private clinics, with a Day-30 target of 1–2 paying pilot clinics.

### Idea Reality MCP

The deep validation report scored the idea **68/100**. It found strong market demand but high competition, with generic CRM/automation features largely commoditized. It recommended a narrower, more defensible starting point rather than a broad "AI growth operating system."

## 2. What the business is

The PDF positions the offer as **Clinic Growth & Conversion** rather than generic digital marketing. The commercial journey is:

**Qualified enquiries → booked appointments → attended appointments → clinic revenue → retention → referrals.**

The core service phases are Diagnose, Fix, Acquire, and Report.

## 3. Initial target market

- Dermatology / aesthetic dermatology
- Mumbai
- Owner-led private clinics
- Initial goal: 1–2 paying pilot clinics by Day 30

The PDF says that if dermatology does not show enough commercial pain, dental or IVF can be tested next.

## 4. What the PDF asks the company to do

### Week 1 — Intelligence

- Define the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP).
- Map 30 agencies/companies serving Indian doctors/clinics.
- Build the first 100 Mumbai dermatology clinic records.
- Mystery-shop 20 clinics.
- Audit 20 A-tier clinics for website/Google presence and booking friction.
- Make a Day-7 Go / Change / Kill decision.

### Week 2 — Build the machine

Create the repeatable offer, website, audit template, outreach system, CRM fields, proposal and reporting templates, compliance checklist, and prospect queue.

### Week 3 — Sales

Generate qualified opportunities, conduct founder sales calls, perform clinic audits, and prepare proposals.

### Week 4 — Close and deliver

Target 1–2 paying pilot clinics, run a 90-day pilot structure, measure operational KPIs, and decide whether to scale, change the niche/offer, or stop.

## 5. Users / actors identified from the source

### Clinic side

- Owner / doctor
- Clinic operations staff

The exact software permission model is **not specified in the PDF** and remains to be decided.

### Internal company side

The PDF explicitly defines Founder and Intern responsibilities. The intern owns research, database work, competitor intelligence, clinic audits, outreach, CRM, follow-ups, meeting preparation, documentation, reporting and SOPs, but does not own medical advice, clinical claims, pricing negotiation, compliance decisions, client strategy, advertising approvals or final sales calls.

- WhatsApp workflow
- Reviews

- Proposal / onboarding

## 7. Important business KPIs

The PDF's dashboard includes acquisition, conversion, business and retention metrics including search visibility, qualified enquiries, cost per qualified enquiry where applicable, response time, contact rate, booking rate, appointment rate, no-show rate, attended appointments, new patients, service mix, reliable revenue attribution, reviews, repeat visits, reactivation and referrals.

## 8. Compliance constraints

The source explicitly prohibits guarantees of medical outcomes or patient numbers, fake testimonials/reviews, unauthorized patient photographs or identifiable patient information, unapproved medical claims, fake patient enquiries, unapproved use of doctor credentials, non-compliant healthcare advertising, careless use of patient data in AI tools, unnecessary health-data collection, and unmeasurable ROI promises.

The PDF also recommends data minimization and a Lead ID → source → enquiry → appointment → attended model rather than collecting extensive medical information.

## 9. MCP findings

The Idea Reality deep report found:

- Reality score: **68/100**.
- Market demand: **80/100**.
- Competition: **40/100**.
- Differentiation: **55/100**.
- Technical feasibility: **75/100**.
- Founder opportunity: **70/100**.

It identified commoditized areas such as basic lead capture, generic WhatsApp replies/reminders, standard CRM workflows, review requests, basic analytics, and email/SMS automation.

It highlighted potential whitespace in proactive patient reactivation, contextual missed-call recovery, deep integration with niche dental software, workflow optimization, and AI-powered upsell/cross-sell.

## 10. Current interpretation

The strongest interpretation is:

> The company should prove a narrow, measurable clinic-growth workflow first, rather than immediately building a huge generic CRM or "operating system."

This is an **interpretation**, not a statement directly made by the PDF.

## 11. Current development principle

**Understand → model data → define workflow → define V1 → prototype → validate → build functional MVP → add AI/integrations → pilot and measure.**

## 12. Open decisions

- Exact V1 feature set.
- Whether the prototype needs both an internal company workspace and clinic workspace.
- Exact user/permission model.
- Exact data schema.
- Which workflow is the first monetizable wedge.
- Whether the prototype uses real public clinic research data, synthetic operational data, or both.
